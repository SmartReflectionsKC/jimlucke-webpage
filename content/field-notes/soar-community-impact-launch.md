A few weeks ago, we were exploring a straightforward question: **How can a church understand the disability community living right around its campus—and use that information to start a meaningful conversation about ministry?**

Today, that idea is a working platform.

On October 17, 2026, we completed the production launch of [SOAR Community Impact](https://impact.soarspecialneeds.org/), a tool developed with SOAR Special Needs to help churches see local community needs and make a stronger, better-informed case for disability ministry.

## Start with the community around your church

A church leader can search for a church, select a geographic radius, and explore U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey estimates of people living with disabilities nearby. The application brings geographic data and demographic estimates together in a way that is easier to understand and put to work.

It's not about promising perfect numbers. These are statistical estimates, and we work to show their limitations honestly. The goal is to give church teams a credible starting point when they ask, *Who in our community might we not be reaching?*

## Turning insight into a leadership conversation

The milestone I'm particularly excited about is the new **Church Leadership Community Report**. A church can request a personalized two-page PDF with its local demographic snapshot, a clearly labeled ministry opportunity planning scenario, and practical first steps for becoming more welcoming.

For our Grace Church — South Overland Park pilot example, the five-mile report estimated 126,806 residents and 9,039 people with disabilities, using 2024 ACS five-year data. The report also presents a modeled outreach scenario rather than confusing SOAR's planning assumptions with Census measurements.

We successfully tested the full live process: generate the PDF, deliver it by email, and verify delivery. A secure SOAR staff dashboard now gives the organization visibility into legitimate report activity and follow-up interest.

## Built for usefulness, not just technology

This project combines location search, Census data, geographic analysis, report generation, cloud infrastructure, and careful privacy controls. There is a lot of technology underneath, but the value is much simpler: **Give people who care about inclusion something practical they can carry into a leadership meeting.**

It also reflects something I've come to believe after more than three decades in technology leadership. Good systems matter most when they help people do something that matters.

For SOAR, that means equipping churches to welcome individuals with disabilities and their families—not simply showing another dashboard.

## What comes next

The initial platform and Leadership Reports are live. Our next work is focused on responsible operations, clear documentation for SOAR leadership, and future opportunities to strengthen the church directory and connect ministries with resources.

I'm grateful to work alongside Dr. Stephen “Doc” Hunsley and the SOAR team on something with the potential to help churches far beyond Kansas City.
